Still Stuck on RAG?

Jan 9 2025 · 7 min read · Shubham

Before we explore advanced concepts like Adaptive RAG and Speculative RAG, let's first understand how basic RAG works. RAG, or Retrieval-Augmented Generation, is a method that enhances the capabilities of LLMs by integrating external, non-parametric knowledge. Instead of relying solely on internal parameters, RAG retrieves relevant documents from a knowledge base and uses this information to generate accurate, grounded responses.

In essence, RAG operates as a straightforward chain of steps:

  • A query is sent to a retriever.
  • The retriever fetches relevant documents.
  • These documents are combined with the query and passed to the LLM to generate an answer.

Here's the thing, though - it's a rigid, literal process. You're feeding documents directly into the model without corrections or deeper interpretation. Consider it: would a human simply regurgitate raw information without context or adjustments? Definitely not. This rigidity creates room for limitations.

Limitations of Basic RAG Systems

While foundational RAG systems are helpful, they're far from perfect. Here are some key challenges:

  • Hallucination - RAG systems often generate information that doesn't exist or is unsupported by retrieved documents. Particularly problematic in fields like medicine or law.
  • Lack of domain-specific precision - RAG doesn't naturally adapt to specialized domains. Without a domain-specific tailoring mechanism, RAG risks retrieving irrelevant or inaccurate content.
  • Struggles with complexity - handling complex, multi-turn conversations or multi-step queries isn't RAG's forte. Maintaining context across interactions is tricky, often leading to incomplete or disjointed responses.

Evolving Beyond Basic Chains to Flows

Here's where Corrective RAG (CRAG) and Adaptive RAG enter the picture.

Corrective RAG (CRAG)

CRAG introduces two key components that dramatically improve the process:

  • A Retrieval Evaluator
  • Knowledge Refinement

Together, these allow CRAG to filter, correct, and refine information before generating a response.

How CRAG works

A Retrieval Evaluator reviews the retrieved documents for relevance and reliability, categorizing them as:

  • Correct - use directly
  • Incorrect - initiate broader web searches
  • Ambiguous - initiate broader web searches

For knowledge refinement, CRAG employs a decompose-then-recompose approach:

  1. Identify key information and filter out irrelevant details.
  2. Recompose the relevant parts into a more cohesive and usable format.

Pay attention that during generation, the input depends on the labeling outcome - what gets passed to the LLM varies based on whether retrieval was correct, incorrect, or ambiguous.

Adaptive RAG: Flexibility in Action

Where CRAG focuses on refining retrieval, Adaptive RAG introduces dynamic flexibility based on the complexity of the query. Think of it as RAG with a brain - it adjusts its approach based on the task at hand.

The query complexity classifier

At the heart of Adaptive RAG is the query complexity classifier - a smaller, specialized LLM that evaluates the complexity of incoming queries and categorizes them as:

  • Simple - questions answerable directly by the LLM (e.g., "What is the capital of France?").
  • Moderate - queries requiring a single step of retrieval (e.g., "When was Michael Phelps born?").
  • Complex - multi-step queries that involve reasoning across multiple documents (e.g., "What is the currency in Billy Giles' birthplace?").

Strategy selection

Based on the classifier's output, Adaptive RAG dynamically selects the retrieval strategy:

  • No Retrieval - for simple queries, responses are generated without external data.
  • Single-Step Retrieval - for moderate queries, a straightforward retrieval process is applied.
  • Multi-Step Retrieval - for complex queries, multiple retrieval iterations refine the response.

By tailoring the retrieval process, Adaptive RAG optimizes performance - reducing unnecessary steps for simple tasks and ensuring accuracy for intricate queries.

Speculative RAG: The Specialist and the Verifier

Finally, we have Speculative RAG, which adds another layer of sophistication by introducing a dual-system approach:

  • Specialist RAG Drafter - a smaller, more efficient LLM designed specifically for reasoning over retrieved documents.
  • Generalist RAG Verifier - a full-fledged LLM that reviews drafts and selects the best one.

How Speculative RAG works

  1. The retriever fetches relevant documents.
  2. Documents are split into subsets and passed to the specialist drafter.
  3. The drafter generates multiple response drafts with rationales - in parallel.
  4. The generalist verifier reviews these drafts and selects the one with the highest confidence score.

For example

Answering: "Which actress played Doralee Rhodes in the 1980 film Nine to Five?"

  • Retrieved documents include details about both the movie and its 2010 musical adaptation.
  • The specialist drafter generates multiple drafts based on retrieved content.
  • The generalist verifier identifies drafts related to the musical as inaccurate and assigns them lower confidence scores.
  • The final answer, based on the movie, is selected and returned.

Speculative RAG excels in speed and precision by offloading intensive reasoning to the specialist drafter and reserving the generalist verifier for validation.

Shouldn't these be combined?

Yep. The idea of understanding ideas is that you can use them, combine them, modify them, or reject them. CRAG's evaluator could plug into Adaptive RAG's routing logic. Speculative RAG's parallel drafting could sit inside CRAG's refinement step. The boundaries are more guidelines than walls.

If you've been stuck with basic RAG systems, these three - CRAG, Adaptive RAG, Speculative RAG - are worth understanding. Each addresses something specific: CRAG fixes bad retrieval, Adaptive RAG avoids unnecessary retrieval, Speculative RAG makes generation faster and more accurate. Together they cover most of the failure modes of vanilla RAG.
