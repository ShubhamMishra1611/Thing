Making Notes

Apr 15 2025 · 5 min read · Shubham

My exams just ended, and the concepts are freshest in my mind. The subject was Indian Philosophy, and I covered the Bhagavad Gita. So, I'm just going to dump everything here - raw, unfiltered - so future me can revisit it if needed.

Alright, let's go.

The question starts with: What does it mean to "be"?

The Gita says, "Well, you're not the body." Okay... so, the mind? Nah. Emotions? Nope. You are the soul.

Alright then.

What is the Soul (Atman)?

This is where things get interesting. The concepts of Atman (soul) and Brahman (ultimate reality) go hand in hand, but different schools interpret their interaction differently.

  • In Vishishtadvaita (Qualified Non-Dualism), Brahman is the ultimate controller, and Atman is under its rule - distinct yet connected.
  • In Advaita (Non-Dualism), everything is Brahman - including the soul. Brahman isn't some external ruler but the fundamental reality itself.

Think of it like a TV screen: the characters on it have their own stories, emotions, and existence, but ultimately, they're just blinking pixels on a screen. The TV was always there - it existed before the characters, and it will remain after them. That's Brahman.

The soul isn't an object like atoms. Atoms are just material things; the soul is what makes me feel like me. This is actually a criticism of Materialism (Carvaka philosophy) - which claims that everything, including consciousness, is just a product of material interactions. The problem? You can't reduce the subject (the experiencer) to an object (the experienced). Materialists say perception is the only valid source of knowledge, rejecting inference and deduction as unreliable. Ironically, though, the real texts of Carvaka were lost, so we only know about it through the criticisms of its rivals.

Anyway, back to the soul. If it's this indestructible, eternal entity, then why do I experience all this chaos - pain, desires, struggle? If I am the soul, shouldn't I feel... still?

The Gita's Answer: You Are a Storm

The Gita describes me as a storm - a swirl of emotion, passion, and intellect. These aren't flaws; they're threads in a fabric.

  • Emotion (bhakti) weaves into devotion.
  • Passion (karma) turns into action.
  • Intellect (jnana) leads to wisdom.

But they're tangled. Yoga (which literally means "union") is the art of untangling - not to escape the storm, but to stand in its eye.

A Yogi isn't some detached, otherworldly figure. A yogi is someone who cooks dinner while humming a prayer, who works hard but doesn't crumble under failure. That's Sthita-prajna - steadiness. Not numbness, but clarity. Seeing the storm and the stillness beneath it.

Action (Karma) and Its Trap

The Gita says I cannot escape action - even breathing is an action. The three Gunas (Tamas, Rajas, Sattva) drive me:

  • Tamas (inertia) drags me down.
  • Rajas (frenzy) pushes me forward.
  • Sattva (balance) whispers, "Do, but don't cling."

That's Niskama Karma - act, but without attachment. Be like an artist painting the sky - pour your heart into it, but be angry when the clouds ruin your painting.

Buddhism also has a take on karma, but that's another rabbit hole.

Knowledge (Jnana) and Devotion (Bhakti)

But how do I actually live like this?

Jnana (knowledge) is the torch. Without it, I'm just a puppet of my impulses. And knowledge isn't just facts; it's seeing the soul behind the skin, the light behind the flame (I don't really understand this light-flame analogy).

Bhakti (devotion) isn't just hymns and rituals - it's action. Love as a verb. It's in washing dishes, listening to a friend, planting trees. The Gita even ranks devotees:

  • The suffering,
  • The curious,
  • The bargainers,
  • And the wise - who see the same divine thread in everything. Vasudevah Sarvam - God is everything.

Gita, Upanishads, and the TV Screen Analogy

The Gita balances two truths:

  • Advaita: I am the wave - rising, falling, crashing.
  • Vishishtadvaita: I am the ocean - vast, still, containing all waves.

Back to the TV screen analogy: The drama of life flickers - birth, war, love, loss. But the screen? Unchanging, silent. That's the real Being. The Absolute wears masks: Krishna the charioteer, the mountain's silence, my neighbor's laughter (seriously I am not even kidding).

Peel the masks away, and what remains? A hum that says, "You were always here."

So - Do I Sit or Sprint?

The Gita grins: Sprint. But with the lightness of someone who knows the ground is an illusion. Tend the garden, kiss the storm, weep when needed - but never forget: I am the sky and the lightning.

Yoga isn't a pose; it's the art of holding contradictions until they melt into a single truth:

"I am the thread, the loom, and the cloth."

And Finally... A Thought Experiment

What if, one day, you wake up - and your parents scold you for playing some Life Simulator too much? The game? Your entire existence so far.

Would you live differently?

Anyways, that's all. This was just for note-making purposes - a way to store my thoughts before they fade. There's a lot of lore left undiscovered, incomplete quests, hidden side missions. And if I've learned anything from games, it's this:

The side quests always make the main story richer. (Seriously the most random line I can think of to end this article)
